Effort Rules (Simple)

  • RPE target: stay between 2 and 4 out of 10 for almost all work.
  • Pain target: stay between 0 and 3 out of 10 during and after training.
  • Breathing rule: if you cannot speak a full sentence, effort is too high for this phase.
  • Hypermobility rule: no deep stretching to end range, no bouncing, no forced positions.
  • Shoulder rule: keep elbow close to body and arm below shoulder height for first 4-6 weeks.
What is RPE (effort out of 10)?
0 = complete rest, 1-2 = very easy, 3-4 = easy to moderate, 5-6 = hard, 7-8 = very hard, 9-10 = near maximal. For this plan, stay mostly at RPE 2-4.
How to score pain out of 10:
0 = no pain, 1-2 = mild awareness, 3 = noticeable but manageable and settles quickly, 4 = too much for this plan (reduce range/reps now), 5+ or sharp pain = stop exercise.
Minimum win is consistency. A 5-minute session done safely is a successful session.

Build Block Effort Rules (Weeks 13-24)

These apply only once you reach week 13 and start the Build tab. Weeks 1-12 keep the gentler rules above.

  • Effort may rise: strength work can reach RPE 4-6/10 - but always stop a set with 2-3 good reps still left in the tank. Never grind to failure.
  • Pain cap does NOT change: still 0-3/10 during and after. 4/10 means reduce load or range now; 5+ or sharp pain means stop that exercise.
  • How to add load (double-progression): when you can do the top of the rep range on all sets with good form and pain at 3/10 or less, next time add either 1-2 reps or the smallest weight step - not both.
  • Weights stay light-to-moderate: most work uses light weights. You should not be reaching for the heaviest dumbbells you own - the heaviest pair is a ceiling to stay well under, not a target.
  • Affected side rule: if one arm is a surgery side, a previously injured joint, or has any swelling history, start that side lighter and one step easier, progress it slower, and only move up when it is fully pain-free.
  • Swelling check: after upper-body or carrying work, glance at both hands/forearms. New puffiness, tightness or heaviness on one side means stop that exercise and let it settle before loading again; if it persists, get it checked.
  • Back-safe loading: whenever you hold a weight, keep the back in its normal neutral line and hinge from the hips. Do not round and lift at the same time under load.
  • Deload weeks: weeks 16 and 20 are deliberately lighter (fewer sets, less weight). Week 24 is a re-test. These are planned - do not skip them to push on.
Golden rule for this block: progress should be boring and small. One extra rep, or one small weight step, every week or two - with calm next-day symptoms - is exactly right.

Warm-up

Diaphragmatic Breathing (Supine or Seated)

1-2 rounds x 60-90 sec

Very Easy
  • Start: Lie on a mat with knees bent and feet flat, or sit with back supported.
  • Hand position: One hand on chest, one hand on lower belly.
  • Breathe in: Inhale through nose for about 4 seconds so belly rises first.
  • Breathe out: Exhale through mouth for about 6 seconds; keep jaw and shoulders relaxed.
  • Stop if dizzy: Pause. Roll to one side before sitting up so shoulders and neck stay supported.

Targets: Diaphragm, deep core support muscles, and nervous-system down-regulation.

Why this is in your plan: Breathing control lowers stress response and muscle guarding before movement.

How this helps you: Makes sessions feel safer, lowers tension, and improves tolerance on low-energy days.

Cat-Cow (Small Range)

1-2 sets x 6 slow reps

Back Mobility

Targets: Spinal mobility (thoracic and lumbar segments) and gentle trunk control.

Why this is in your plan: Slow segmental motion reduces stiffness without high joint load.

How this helps you: Helps loosen upper/lower back and prepares you for the rest of the session.

Build Block - Weeks 13 to 24

Current session length goal: 22-30 min per session (updates with the week you pick on the Start tab).

You have finished the 12-week foundation. This block adds gentle strength: the same movements you know, now with a little weight and a touch more effort, progressed slowly.

How to use this block

  • Keep the same rhythm: 3 sessions a week, rotating Build Day A, B, C below. These replace Today A/B/C from week 13 onward.
  • Read the Build Block Effort Rules on the Start tab first. The short version: effort may reach 4-6/10, but pain stays 0-3/10 and you always leave 2-3 reps in reserve.
  • Add weight by the double-progression rule: hit the top of the rep range with good form and calm symptoms, then add a rep or the smallest weight step - not both.
  • Deload on weeks 16 and 20 (lighter on purpose). Re-test on week 24.
  • If a loaded version flares something, drop back to the week 1-12 version of that move for a week, then try again lighter.
One arm may be a surgery side, a previously injured joint, or prone to swelling. On that side: start lighter, progress slower, and check your hands/forearms for swelling after upper-body and carrying work. Stop that exercise if swelling appears and let it settle.

The 12-Week Arc

  • Weeks 13-14 - Introduce: add a light weight to the lower-body basics; learn the loaded versions. Upper body stays as before.
  • Week 15 - Build: begin the double-progression rule.
  • Week 16 - Deload: lighter week; consolidate.
  • Weeks 17-19 - Build: progress reps or small loads gradually.
  • Week 20 - Deload: lighter week; consolidate.
  • Weeks 21-23 - Peak: your hardest tolerated variations and loads, still well short of maximal.
  • Week 24 - Re-test & decide: compare to week 12, then repeat the block harder or hold at maintenance.

Week 24 Re-Test & Decide

Repeat the two foundation tests and compare to week 12.

  • 30-second sit-to-stand: count full stands in 30 seconds.
  • 5-minute walk comfort: same route and time of day; note effort (RPE) and how you feel 10 minutes after.
  • Also note: the weight used comfortably on each main move, any exercise that still flares, and any swelling.

Then choose the next step

  • Progressing well, symptoms calm: repeat the Build block from week 13, starting a little heavier.
  • Tired or symptoms creeping up: hold at a maintenance version - 2 sessions a week at current loads - for a few weeks.
  • Plateau or a nagging issue: a good point for a physiotherapy check-in before pushing further.
Keep the same red flags as the rest of the plan: stop and seek medical review for chest pain, faintness, severe breathlessness, new neurological symptoms, sharp joint pain, or persistent one-sided arm swelling.

Flare Day Algorithm (Green / Yellow / Red)

Green Day: Normal symptoms. Do full planned session.
Yellow Day: More fatigue/pain than usual but safe. Do 50% volume (half sets, slower tempo).
Red Day: Significant flare or warning signs. Skip strength. Do only 3-5 min breathing + gentle mobility, then rest.

3-Minute Rescue Routine

1) Breathing Reset

60 sec

  • Start: Sit with back supported, both feet on floor, hands relaxed on thighs.
  • Breathe in: Inhale through nose for 4 seconds.
  • Breathe out: Exhale through mouth for 6 seconds (long and gentle).
  • Tempo: Repeat for 6-8 slow breaths (about 60 seconds).
  • Safety: Stop if dizzy, chest pain, or unusual breathlessness appears.

Targets: Breathing mechanics and autonomic calming response.

Why this is in your plan: This rapidly lowers arousal and helps symptom control during flare days.

How this helps you: Gives you a safe "reset" when energy or symptoms are unstable.

2) Seated Pelvic Tilt

6 reps

  • Start: Sit tall near front of chair, feet flat, hands on thighs.
  • Move 1: Exhale and gently tuck pelvis so low back flattens slightly.
  • Move 2: Inhale and gently tip pelvis forward into a tiny arch.
  • Tempo: 6 slow reps, no forcing range.
  • Safety: Stop for sharp pain, radiating pain, or numbness.

Targets: Gentle lumbar-pelvic mobility and low-level core activation.

Why this is in your plan: Keeps back moving with minimal load when full exercise is not possible.

How this helps you: Prevents stiffness from escalating on bad symptom days.

3) Gentle March Seated

45-60 sec

  • Start: Sit tall with back supported, hold chair sides if needed.
  • Move: Alternate lifting knees in a small range, one at a time.
  • Breathe: Keep normal breathing and relaxed shoulders.
  • Tempo: Slow and steady for 45-60 seconds.
  • Safety: Stop immediately if symptoms escalate.

Targets: Hip flexors, circulation, and very low-intensity cardio system.

Why this is in your plan: Maintains blood flow and movement without impact or shoulder load.

How this helps you: Supports conditioning and energy without provoking joints.

Stop + Seek Medical Review If

  • New chest symptoms, severe shortness of breath, or fainting sensation.
  • Joint swelling/hot joint worsening after sessions for more than 24-48 hours.
  • Back or shoulder pain sharply worsens and does not settle with load reduction.
  • Any symptom that feels different from baseline disease pattern.

What To Track Each Session







Vitals and Objective Progress (Simple)

  • Resting heart rate (RHR): log in the morning, before caffeine, at least 4 days/week.
  • Blood pressure (BP): log at least 3 days/week and before harder days.
  • Sleep: log hours slept + sleep quality (1-5).
  • Energy and pain: log daily energy (1-10) and pain (0-10).
Weekly 5-minute walk test (same day/time each week):
Warm up 2-3 minutes easy. Then walk 5 minutes at 4.0 km/h (treadmill) or brisk flat walk equivalent. Record: average HR, peak HR, RPE, pain during test, and 10-minute recovery feeling.
BP safety reminder: if resting BP is around 180/110 or higher, skip training and seek medical advice. If around 180/120 with symptoms (chest pain, breathlessness, neuro symptoms), seek urgent care.

Expected Progress If Consistent

  • Weeks 1-2: learns form, tolerates very short sessions.
  • Weeks 3-6: less stiffness, slightly better daily energy, improved confidence.
  • Weeks 7-10: can handle longer sessions, better movement control and posture.
  • Weeks 11-12: stronger baseline and readiness for next progressive block.
Progress may be non-linear. Consistency and smart recovery management matter more than perfect weeks.
